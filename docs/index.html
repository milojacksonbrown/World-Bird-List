The count

Where in the world

Each country you've birded in is shaded by how much of its birdlife you've seen, out of all the species ever recorded there on eBird. Hover over or tap a country for details, and click it to see its states and provinces.

The bars show, for each region of the world, how many of the birds that live there or pass through you've seen, wherever you saw them. Rare vagrants are left out.

In the region bars, each country counts towards one region. Russia, Egypt and Indonesia, which span two, are placed by continent.

    Through the years

    The top charts show your lifers each year, and in each month of the year (all years together), so the seasons stand out. The bottom charts count every species you recorded, lifer or not, with a line for your checklists as a guide to how much birding you did.

    Latest lifers

    Every species you've seen, newest lifer first. Search for a bird, or click any of them to open its fact file.

      Difficulty

      The pictures are AI illustrations (from huashu-bird-sticker), so a few may not match the bird exactly. Typed answers can use any of the bird's names; capitals don't matter, and small spelling slips are forgiven. Recordings come from xeno-canto, each credited to its recordist. "Quiz me on" works like Ask your list: only your request is sent, through Lyrebird to an AI model, which turns it into a checked search of your lifers.

      Explore

      What could you add to your list right now? Pick a place to see the birds reported there in the last 30 days that you haven't seen yet. Click a country on the map, then a state or province (and, in some countries, a county), or use the menus below.

      Recent sightings come from eBird (Cornell Lab of Ornithology), fetched through Lyrebird and kept for an hour. Where eBird marks a place as private, its name is left out.

      Names to check

      These eBird names don't match an AviList species yet, usually because the two checklists disagree about what counts as a species. They're left out of your totals until you fix them.

      Suggest a match first compares the name with AviList on your own computer. If that's not enough to be sure, it asks an AI model (through Lyrebird) to choose from a shortlist, sending only the bird names involved. Nothing changes until you accept.
