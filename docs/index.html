A field guide to your birds

Life List

Upload your eBird data and see every species you've seen, matched to AviList, the global checklist of the world's birds.

Where do I get my eBird data?
  1. On eBird, go to My eBird ▸ Download My Data and submit the request.
  2. eBird emails you a link to a .zip file. Download it and unzip it.
  3. Upload the MyEBirdData.csv file inside. It's read in your browser and never uploaded anywhere.

The count

Where in the world

The countries you've birded in, shaded by the share of the species ever recorded there (eBird) that you've seen. The bars show how many of the birds regularly found in each region (breeding, wintering or on migration) you've seen, wherever you saw them. Rare vagrants aren't included. Tap or hover over a country for details, and click a birded country to see its states and provinces.

For the region figures, each country belongs to one region; countries spanning two (Russia, Egypt, Indonesia) go by continent.

      Ask your list

      Ask a question in plain English. The answer comes from your own data, and you're shown how the question was understood.

      Try:

      Your question (and nothing else) goes through Lyrebird to an AI model (OpenAI's GPT-OSS on Groq, or Google's Gemini as a backup), which turns it into a search. The search is checked against what's allowed, then run here in your browser. Your data never leaves it. (Free AI services may keep what's sent, so don't include anything personal.)

      Names to check

      These eBird names don't match an AviList species yet, often because the two checklists disagree about what counts as a species. They aren't counted above until you fix them.

      Suggest a match first compares the name with AviList on your computer. If that isn't conclusive, it asks an AI model (through Lyrebird) to choose from a shortlist, sending only the bird names involved. Nothing changes until you accept.
